/goal

Implement the next Phase 12 vertical slice: Stage 4 revision-aware invalidation scheduling and frame correlation across ordinary Node/OpenGL and Wasm/WebGPU applications.

Primary outcome:
A settled, inactive application stops constructing and submitting native frames. A committed publication, visible imperative change, input event, resource completion, screenshot request or real activity deadline wakes rendering promptly. Active interaction follows the backend/display cadence without the current fixed 30 Hz policy. Invalidation arriving while the renderer decides to sleep, constructs a frame or submits work cannot be lost.

Every successfully submitted frame identifies the coherent native structural revision and invalidation generation represented by its draw data. Multiple publications may be covered by one later frame; a synchronous publication acknowledgment still means applied state and cleanup, not physical presentation. Preserve the complete Stage 3 publication/lifetime path while replacing its rendering policy. Deliver the scheduler, producer integration, correlation, deterministic tests, real-runtime gates and comparable measurements together.

Alpha compatibility policy:
XFrames has been alpha from the outset and has made no stability commitment. Backward compatibility is not a default acceptance requirement: retain compatibility only for an identified consumer or migration need that justifies its cost. Do not preserve the fixed render loop or add a parallel scheduler merely to retain earlier alpha behavior. Keep the Stage 3 schema-v2 publication contract, public React components, imperative widget APIs and initialization/ready callbacks as scope choices for this slice, not a general stability promise. Make any narrowly necessary diagnostic or activity-policy API change explicit, migrate callers and tests, and reject unsupported contracts rather than keeping a second implementation. Do not restore schema v1 or the removed immediate structural exports.

Deliverables:

1. Establish one shared invalidation and frame-completion model.
   - Define monotonic invalidation generation, the generation covered by a completed frame, structural revision represented by that frame, dirty state and active/deadline reasons. Structural revision orders structural publications; resource/input/imperative invalidation must not increment it or create extra structural transactions.
   - A successful publication, including accepted empty/no-op publication, requests rendering at publication scope. Rejected preflight and abandoned Fabric work must not create a successful-publication invalidation. Define the terminal wake/diagnostic behavior of quarantined surfaces without repeatedly drawing partial content as healthy.
   - Publish work before its wake and synchronize generation capture with the state it describes. A renderer must never mark generation N covered if its captured tree/resources exclude work represented by N. Capture the native revision with draw-state construction under the existing visibility boundary, not by reading the latest revision after submission.
   - Prove the transition into waiting and the transition from frame submission back to clean are race-safe. Work arriving before sleep, between checking state and waiting, during rendering, or after the draw snapshot but before completion remains pending or wakes another iteration. A Boolean cleared after rendering or an unproved generation check is insufficient.
   - Preserve structural dispatch -> serialized subject -> hierarchy -> elements lock order. Scheduler wakeups, backend callbacks and frame observers must not introduce tree-to-dispatch lock inversion, recursively acquire tree locks, or invoke application JS under them.
   - Keep scheduling correctness available with expensive diagnostics disabled. Define initialization, enable/disable, counter exhaustion and runtime disposal behavior; diagnostic resets must not reset ordering or lose pending work. Keep counters lossless across bindings.
   - Bound scheduler reasons, active registrations, deadlines, pending notifications and correlation records. Coalescing wakeups/frame opportunities is permitted; discarding committed state or underlying imperative data is not.

2. Replace desktop periodic rendering with event/deadline scheduling.
   - Integrate the shared model into the actual ImGuiRenderer/Node path. Wait indefinitely when clean and inactive; use the nearest genuine deadline when one exists. Dirty work and active interaction must not inherit a fixed 30 Hz wait. Do not leave a periodic rescue render that conceals missing producers.
   - Preserve and correctly chain GLFW/ImGui backend callbacks for mouse motion/buttons, wheel, keyboard/text, focus, window refresh/exposure, resize and content scale. A real input event must wake a clean renderer and reach ImGui/event dispatch without replacing required backend handling.
   - Cover first frame after ready, framebuffer/window-size changes, minimize/restore, zero-sized framebuffer and window close while idle. When drawing is temporarily impossible, retain pending state without a busy retry loop or claiming a successful submitted frame.
   - Process render-thread work such as image/texture uploads before the corresponding frame can be marked covered. A queue processed incrementally must keep remaining work scheduled until drained; one initial wake must not strand later jobs.
   - Use monotonic time and an explicit policy for ImGui DeltaTime after a long idle interval. Track real deadlines for cursor blink, key repeat, tooltip/hover delays, drag/scroll/navigation activity and widget timers. Do not keep all focused or hovered widgets permanently active merely to avoid the audit.
   - Preserve screenshot capture and normal shutdown/ready behavior. Screenshot requests made while clean must complete or fail explicitly; a scheduler callback must not retain or access a destroyed renderer/window.

3. Replace the browser 30 Hz policy with dirty/active requestAnimationFrame scheduling.
   - Use the same invalidation/coverage semantics through the actual Wasm entry point and ReactNativeWrapper. Follow browser animation-frame cadence while dirty or active, and skip ImGui preparation, native draw construction and WebGPU submission when clean.
   - Prefer pausing/cancelling the animation-frame loop when inactive. A lightweight browser callback that only checks state is acceptable if justified and measured separately from rendered frames; it must not retain the old fixed-frequency native render loop.
   - Browser input, JS publications/imperative calls, async resource callbacks, resize/scale and visibility restoration must restart or schedule rendering after the loop becomes clean. Preserve the existing bounded deferred native-to-JS event drain.
   - Handle hidden tabs, zero-sized/minimized surfaces, visibility return and delayed browser callbacks without spinning or losing pending invalidation. Do not promise a frame while the platform suspends rendering; retain its revision/generation and deliver it when renderable again.
   - A skipped/failed surface acquisition or WebGPU submission must not advance completed-frame coverage. Define bounded retry on recoverable conditions and honest terminal behavior for unrecoverable device failure; broad device-recovery redesign is outside scope.
   - Teardown, Strict Mode wrapper reuse and ordinary remount must not accumulate animation callbacks, timers, event listeners or stale renderer references. No second browser-only state/ownership engine.

4. Integrate every existing invalidation producer and time-dependent consumer.
   - Produce a source-level inventory mapping each state-changing path to its invalidation reason, owning runtime/widget lifetime, wake mechanism, queued render work and regression test. Cover structural publications, all visible imperative widget paths, native interaction, window/style/font/debug changes and screenshots.
   - Cover Image fetch/decode/upload, desktop image jobs, Map tile completion/upload and visible prefetch progress, and QuickJS/Lua/Janet Canvas script/data/texture completion. Exercise actual completion paths after the renderer has become idle; unrelated mouse movement or a periodic frame must not rescue them.
   - Account for existing work that advances only during Render: Map's zoom debounce/fetch deadline, queued Canvas scripts/textures, ImGui layout/interaction settling and time-dependent drawing. Define a bounded settling policy based on real pending work so a first visible frame does not require a later unrelated event.
   - Define the Canvas activity policy explicitly. Time-dependent scripts must continue advancing while active; static content must have a way to settle. Any narrowly necessary explicit redraw/continuous-activity control belongs to this slice and must be documented, migrated and tested. Do not infer arbitrary script time dependencies or silently freeze existing animations.
   - Keep asynchronous completion and active/deadline registrations tied to the correct runtime and widget lifetime. Removal, replacement or shutdown must release them; late completion cannot target a reused native ID, wake a destroyed runtime or leave a permanent active reason. Preserve registrations/resources across a same-ID move.
   - Fix resource-lifetime or synchronization defects narrowly where needed for safe producer integration, including callbacks that currently capture a destroyed widget. Do not expand into a general resource loader, total-order command protocol or unrelated asynchronous shutdown rewrite.
   - Queries and ordinary diagnostic polling must not invalidate the renderer. Enabling diagnostics, refreshing debug state or explicitly requesting a frame may schedule bounded work under a documented policy; a diagnostic test must not keep its own idle workload alive.

5. Expose bounded, truthful revision-to-frame correlation and measured costs.
   - Extend shared frame/query diagnostics and binding types with frame identity, captured structural revision, covered invalidation generation, current pending state and relevant reason/deadline counters. Preserve the distinction between live applied state, constructed draw data, backend submission and any observable swap/presentation endpoint.
   - Define completion as a submitted frame containing revision >= the requested revision, with state/ownership assertions where required. Several publications may share one frame; an intermediate tree need not be individually displayed. Record first inclusion where measured rather than pretending one frame corresponds to exactly one commit.
   - Imperative PlotBar/Table data does not advance structural revision. Correlate it using generation coverage and/or instrumented sample identity with native application times, so a frame carrying the right structural revision but stale data cannot satisfy a data-frame assertion. Avoid imposing a new public total order on all widget commands.
   - Retain source/application receipt, React request/completeRoot, serialization, native parse/validation/application, invalidation, wake, frame construction and backend submission timing where measurable. Compute durations using one clock each; do not subtract JS and native absolute timestamps without a valid clock relationship.
   - Report p50/p95/p99/maximum, publication/frame coalescing, wake counts, frames constructed/submitted, skipped opportunities, active/deadline reasons, idle CPU and lifetime counts. Distinguish browser callback opportunities from actual native frames; do not invent skipped callbacks when a paused loop produced none.
   - Keep optional histories and latency samples bounded, with explicit truncation/overflow accounting. Report instrumentation overhead and unavailable intervals. No replay subjects containing completed trees, no durable trace recorder, and no claim of GPU completion or physical presentation based on queue submission, glDraw/glfwSwapBuffers return, or a screenshot alone.
   - Migrate diagnostics and smoke wait helpers from arbitrary newer-frame assumptions to revision/generation/sample coverage or a narrowly explicit frame request. A rejected publication or repeated query should not require an unsolicited frame. Preserve unchanged-state/parity assertions without keeping the old periodic loop to satisfy tests.

6. Turn scheduling correctness into native and actual-application regression gates.
   - Add deterministic shared-native scheduler tests with a controllable monotonic clock and coordinated barriers/latches. Cover clean-to-dirty, repeated/coalesced invalidations, each sleep/completion race, deadline creation/cancellation, counter limits, first frame, rejected publication, quarantine, disposal and skipped submission. Sleeps or occasional snapshots cannot establish absence of a lost wakeup.
   - Keep Stage 3's deterministic visibility test through real subject delivery. Add a revision/coverage race proving that a publication after frame-state capture cannot be relabeled as included in that earlier frame, and that its work remains scheduled.
   - Run equivalent fixtures through current-source Node/OpenGL and Wasm/WebGPU after a proved settled/idle state. Independently stimulate publication, imperative data, native input, delayed resources, timer/deadline activity, screenshot capture and resize/restore. Require semantic state, generation/revision coverage, timely wake and return to inactivity after each finite activity.
   - Use deterministic local assets and controlled delayed resource responses for Image/Map/Canvas gates, not public tile servers or timing luck. Cover multiple queued jobs, failure/completion, removal before completion and time-dependent Canvas/Map behavior on each supported backend.
   - Prove active work is not governed by the former 30 Hz policy using deterministic scheduling assertions and measured real-runtime cadence on declared adapters. Software rendering and display limits must be reported rather than turned into misleading universal FPS assertions.
   - Preserve all ten XF-LIFE gates, rejected/failed JS completion behavior, real binding parity, one-call/one-revision publication accounting, ordinary wrapper/Strict Mode lifecycle, populated data, stale handles/events and diagnostics-disabled behavior.
   - Re-run 1,000 ordinary lifecycle cycles with pending/abandoned work and native same-ID moves on each runtime. Assert zero growth in all ten original lifetime fields and in new scheduler callbacks/listeners/timers/deadlines/active owners/correlation state after acknowledged unmount and a submitted covering empty frame. No process exit, forced GC or destroyed survivors as cleanup substitutes.
